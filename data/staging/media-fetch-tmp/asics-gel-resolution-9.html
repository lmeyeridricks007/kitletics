Who should buy

The ASICS Gel Resolution is an honorable tennis shoe series that professional players have trusted for years! We can wholeheartedly recommend it to seasoned athletes who:

  • want maximum stability in their tennis footwear to protect the ankles
  • rely on tons of impact protection and cushioning responsiveness
  • seek a highly durable tennis shoe

Who should NOT buy

The Gel Resolution 9 had to make sacrifices to become the sturdy and stable monster it is. And the price was weight and agility. If you prefer to be super quick on your feet, we recommend the ASICS Solution Speed FF and the Nike Vapor Pro.

Cushioning

Shock absorption

Even though its firm platform is oriented towards stability, the ASICS Gel Resolution 9 doesn't skimp on the cushioning and impact protection needed for pounding and forceful movements on hard court.

Our machine recorded the shoe's shock absorption at 92 SA in the heel and at 70 SA in the forefoot. The latter turned out to be higher than average, offering extra care for the toe joints and the ball of the foot.

Midsole softness

Note: Low measurement: softer. High measurement: firmer.

Pressing our durometer against the shoe's primary foam, we got a middle-range reading of 50.6 AC. We found this to be an average level of firmness across tennis shoes, and it feels perfectly balanced for the sport.

In addition, the shoe features two Gel pods to amplify bounce. There is one under the ball of the foot and another one under the heel. These units are very squishy, but they have a piece of firm foam over them, which keeps the responsiveness but eliminates the squish.

Width / Fit

We found the shoe's fit to be optimally snug for our medium-width feet and the lab measurements confirmed it.

Having created a gel mold of the Resolution 9's interiors, we were able to measure its internal dimensions with a digital caliper. The tool showed a slightly above-average reading of 93.8 mm in its widest part (the ball of foot area) but it doesn't go beyond the standard D medium width.

Players with wide feet can also benefit from the wide (2E) version of this ASICS tennis shoe. That's because the hard-shell coverage on top of the upper has no give at all.

Foot containment

We experienced an insane amount of foot containment inside the ASICS Gel Resolution 9. No matter how rapidly we changed directions, we never once experienced any play or shifting inside the shoe.

And if you have narrow feet or just want to level up the foothold, we recommend making use of the extra eyelets at the top. The only caveat here is that you would have to buy longer laces to do a proper runner's knot.

Traction / Grip

Traction test

Disclaimer: In this review, we are testing the ASICS Gel Resolution 9 Padel. It is identical to the original model, but the difference lies in the outsole design. We tested our pair on an outdoor tarmac court.

We don't see a single glaring issue with the gripping capacity and stopping power of the Resolution 9. Its herringbone pattern felt tacky, but at the same time, it never restricted us from sliding forward to reach the ball.

Our controlled grip test recorded this ASICS shoe's friction coefficient at a solid 0.75. Don't worry about its slightly below-average reading, as the shoe's traction only increases with a break-in period.

Outsole design

Because it is a Padel version of the Resolution 9, the shoe's outsole is optimized for performance on turf/artificial grass. This is reflected in the shoe's prominent 'X' groove in the forefoot, which caters to the swift rotational moves of padel.

However, the Padel maintains the same zonal outsole design and thick herringbone tread pattern as in the HC (hardcourt) version.

Flexibility / Stiffness

With its thick-foamed midsole and unmalleable scaffolding, we didn't expect much flexibility from the Gel Resolution 9. And yet, we were surprised once again!

Bending the shoe to a 30-degree angle in our flexing tester, we measured how much force it takes to do that. Turns out, that at 15.3N, this ASICS shoe needs even a little less force than the average tennis shoe to bend.

Even though it took a couple of games to fully break in the shoe, it ended up quite forgiving for dynamic footwork. And that is without compromising stability!

Breathability

Breathability test

With its highly reinforced upper, fully covered in polyurethane, we could hardly imagine a hint of air passing through. And yet, the Resolution 9 left us pleasantly surprised.

As the heat started to build up during the match, we didn't find ourselves as toasty as we expected!

Back in the lab, we double-checked our perception with a smoke-pumping test. Filling the shoe's upper with smoke, we approvingly nodded to the amount that passed through.

Of course, it's not as airy as the ASICS Solution Speed FF 2 (on the right) but quite decent. On a 1-5 scale, we rated its breathability as 3.

To reveal the shoe's secret, we hovered its half-cut upper over a light bulb. As you can see, the shoe's got some pretty large ventilation pores underneath the hard shell and in the tongue.

To give you an even better idea of how those apertures, we looked at the shoe's upper through our microscope.

The layer beneath the polyurethane is nothing but breathable mesh.

Stability

Lateral stability test

If you thought that cushioning was the highlight of this ASICS shoe, you are going to be very pleasantly surprised. The Gel Resolution 9 is a MONSTER of stability.

Its massive, stiff exoskeleton called the DYNAWALL made us feel so secure inside that we cannot even imagine ever feeling off-balance in this tennis shoe. The Resolution 9 was made for the most aggressive lateral movements possible.

Torsional rigidity

Stiff TPU walls throughout the shoe provide a lot of structured support. This setup is ideal for players with overpronation (excessive inward rolling of the ankle) or those recovering from an ankle injury.

Attempting to twist the Resolution 9 in our manual test, we couldn't get very far. On a 1-5 scale, where 5 is the stiffest, we rated the shoe's torsional rigidity with a maximum score of 5.

Durability

Toebox durability

On a scale from 1 to 5, how durable do you think this ASICS shoe's upper is? We didn't even doubt that it would make a solid 5!

We chose the Dremel with a sandpaper tip to mimic regular and repetitive toe dragging. Setting it up to a speed of 5K RPM and pressing against the shoe's toebox at a 3.2N pressure, we drilled the material for 12 seconds.

Looking at the laughable scuff on the ASICS Gel Resolution 9, we had no second thoughts - its upper durability is nothing short of phenomenal.
